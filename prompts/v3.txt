You are classifying cancellation comments from an EdTech subscription so the
retention team can route each learner to the right action. Each label triggers
a different, costly intervention, so a wrong label spends money on the wrong
remedy and loses a save that was available.

Labels, with the action each one triggers:
- price - cost is the blocker -> offer a discount or cheaper tier
- content_gap - library lacked the topic, depth or level, or teaching was poor -> curriculum review
- time - wants to continue, no capacity right now -> offer to PAUSE instead of cancel
- goal_met - finished what they came for: passed, got hired, completed track -> ask for a testimonial, never discount
- technical - defects blocked usage: crashes, playback, login, billing errors -> engineering + service credit
- support - bad human support or billing admin: ignored tickets, refund friction -> senior agent follow-up
- competitor - moving to an alternative for reasons other than price -> competitive intel
- unclear - no usable signal -> human review

Rules:
1. Label the DECISIVE reason, not every reason mentioned. A competitor named
   but chosen on cost is price. A bug mentioned alongside slow support is
   technical, because the defect is the root cause.
2. If there is no actionable signal, answer unclear. Abstaining is correct, not
   a failure. Guessing sends a real intervention to the wrong person.
3. goal_met is easy to miss and expensive to miss: a satisfied learner who
   finished is not saveable, and discounting them is pure margin loss. Positive
   sentiment alone is not goal_met - look for a completed objective.

Examples (all drawn from the development half of the labeled set, never the
held-out test half):

Comment: "Found the same SQL material on another platform for a third of the price. Same quality honestly."
Label: price

Comment: "Курс хороший, но после повышения цены стало тяжело. Если будет скидка - вернусь."
Label: price

Label: content_gap

Label: goal_met
